Dylan · Chiang Rai Marathon
Signed up 18 Sep 2025  ·  Raced 7 Feb 2026

Twenty weeks, and most of it in the dark.

Signed up on a Thursday in September. Ran the first session three days later — 15.3 km of 4-minute reps into a Vancouver headwind. What followed: a hemisphere change, 928 km on foot, 2,266 km on the bike, a week lost to a virus, an ankle rolled in a pitch-black warm-up area in Cambodia, a bike crash on a corner he knew well, and seven races — four of which started before 6 a.m., and the last of which started at 9 p.m.

The clock moved before he did

In Vancouver he was an afternoon runner. Six weeks later, in Chiang Mai, the heat had shoved the entire operation into the morning — and the races kept dragging it further, into the dark on both ends. Every dot is one run.

The one thing that never shifted was yoga — median start 5:18 p.m., eleven of twenty-one sessions after five. Whatever the day looked like, it ended on the mat.

Sixteen and a half minutes

Six half marathons in fourteen weeks. Two of them were deliberately not raced, one was run on a rolled ankle, one had 274 m of surprise climbing. The line still went one way.

Three things that went wrong

All three are visible in the chart at the top.

6 – 16 Nov

Food poisoning, then a virus

Dead on Thursday, attempted a threshold 5 km on Friday anyway, then lost the following week outright. Week 7 finished with two runs and 15.6 km — the floor of the block.

7 Dec

The ankle, in the dark

Rolled in the unlit warm-up area before the Angkor Wat half. Raced on it anyway. The week after: two elliptical sessions and run volume cut nearly in half.

5 Jan

Down on a familiar corner

Crashed 27 km into a ride and blamed sand he never saw. Arms shredded, legs untouched. Six days later: a half marathon PR by nine and a half minutes.

What it was run in

Six pairs, 927.7 km. Two pairs of 1080s took two-thirds of it.
